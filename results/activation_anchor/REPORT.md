## Reading (kept narrow)
- **A+C was new** (no feedback-plus-anchor configuration existed on any branch or worktree).
- **Primary, AC vs C (64 fresh images): inconclusive.** Mean latent MSE ratio 1.017 [0.789, 1.294], 24/64 wins, ΔLPIPS +0.003 [−0.004, +0.009].
  The CI is wide (the anchored errors are small and heavy-tailed); it neither shows a feedback benefit on top of anchoring nor establishes equivalence.
- **Feedback alone helps** (A vs Q 0.926 [0.894, 0.955], 56/64). **Measured anchoring dominates:** C vs Q 0.079, AC vs A 0.086 (64/64 each).
- **Correction-specific gains are large against both anchor-only controls:** C vs C_anchor_only 0.135 [0.094, 0.185]; AC vs AC_anchor_only 0.146
  [0.107, 0.193] (call cost and feedback-state updates matched). Feedback still helps without reuse (AC_anchor_only vs C_anchor_only 0.942 [0.895, 0.981]).
- **Interaction:** I = −0.0102 [−0.0153, −0.0056] — feedback's absolute benefit shrinks with anchoring (from 0.0100 without anchors to −0.0002
  [−0.0026, +0.0027] with them). The relative form, [E(AC)/E(C)]/[E(A)/E(Q)] = 1.099 [0.863, 1.396], is unresolved. Since absolute gains naturally shrink
  as baseline error falls (E(C) is 13× smaller than E(Q)), this is not evidence that feedback and anchoring are redundant mechanisms.
- **Persistence (8 dev images, own trajectories):** feedback slightly lowers the lag correlation of the cheap model's error (ρ at lag 1/4: Q 0.877/0.655
  vs A 0.850/0.644; C 0.810/0.530 vs AC 0.782/0.522), and the non-anchor corrected/raw energy is 0.551 (C) vs 0.587 (AC). That is consistent with,
  not proof of, feedback making the remaining error less reusable; cross-arm differences include state-distribution changes.
- Not shown: deployable speed (fake-quant simulation only), generation quality (no FID), other K, β or feedback sizes, or the eight-arm W+A+C design.
