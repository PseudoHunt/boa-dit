# Sample-independent attention in DiT — summary

Generated by `scripts/make_attn_summary.py`. Model: **DiT-XL/2, 256 px, class-conditional ImageNet**, DDIM-50, cfg 1.5, FP weights. Details in `gate1.md`, `gate2.md`, `gate3.md` and `compute.md`; dated log in `../REPORT.md`.

## What was shown

At high-noise steps, a DiT-XL/2 block's attention map is, to a good approximation, a property of the (block, head, step, position) and not of the image. The mean map over 32 calibration images (16 classes × 2 noises) can replace the computed softmax(QKᵀ) for every image. Sharing steps 0–14 costs +0.36 FID-2k, and steps 0–19 cost +1.20 (FP 23.89, same 2,000 seeds). Uniform maps over steps 0–9 cost +20.97, so the shared maps carry real structure, not just near-uniformity. The maps are class-independent (cross-image row cosine at s ≤ 9: same class 0.974 vs different class 0.956) and branch-independent (cond vs uncond mean map 0.998). They are close to translation-invariant: a 31×31 offset kernel per head rebuilds the mean map at cosine 0.983. In images, the kernel form (V3, 6.9 MB for all 20 shared steps) is +0.55 FID-2k from the full per-step maps (+1.75 vs FP).

This shares attention **across samples**. Existing diffusion caching methods (DeepCache, TeaCache, FORA, Δ-DiT, ToCa, and PAB, which broadcasts attention across steps in video DiTs) reuse computation across adjacent steps of one sample, and ToMe-SD merges tokens spatially. As far as we know, the cross-sample axis is untaken.

## Speed: deferred, not claimed

At 256 px (N = 256 tokens) attention is a small part of a DiT block, so sharing k = 20 steps removes only 7.1% of the FLOPs end to end (N = 1024: 8.5%; N = 4096: 11.8%).
 Measured on the A30 at N = 256, a fully shared forward changes latency by -1.0% against fused SDPA (plain PyTorch, no custom kernel).


**No speed claim is made for this model.** The next step is a high-token-count model: **PixArt-Σ at 1024 px (4096 tokens)**. There the N² attention core (QKᵀ, softmax, AV) grows to 38% of a block's FLOPs, up from 4% at 256 tokens, at the same width (C = 1152). That is comparable to the MLP (42%), not a majority. Dense stored maps cannot fit there (16 · 4096² · 2 B = 537 MB per block, branch and step). There the compact V3 kernel is the only viable form.


Open risk for that step: at 256 px the V3 kernel is not compact. The smallest centered square holding 95% of its mass is the full 31×31 in 98% of (window, block, head) cases. A convolution path is cheap at 4096 tokens only if the kernel's support stays bounded as the grid grows; that has to be measured first on PixArt-Σ.


Per-block microbenchmarks (`compute.md` §5; synthetic inputs, A30): at N = 4096 a dense stored map applied to all rows takes 11.1 ms vs 27.3 ms for fused attention, but needs 537 MB per block-step. The V3 convolution takes 783 ms in PyTorch/cuDNN. **A fast kernel-form implementation does not exist yet**; it is a prerequisite for the PixArt-Σ step, along with bounded kernel support.

## Residual-kernel support: 95%-mass metric (held by the reviewer, superseded by the truncation test below)

The reviewer held this verdict. Most of the kernel's cells lie outside 15 × 15, and each is averaged over few token pairs and clipped to non-negative noise, which inflates the 95%-mass side. Kept for the record.

From the saved V3 kernels (`artifacts/V3_kernels_k20.pt`), per (block, head, window), k = 20; κ_res = (κ − floor)₊; side = smallest centered square holding 95% of κ_res's mass (full = 31). Rule: compact iff median side ≤ 9 and ≥ 90% of kernels ≤ 15. Source: `kernel_residual_support.json`.

Under this (held) metric: residual not compact. That reading holds whether the floor is the minimum or the median of κ. On DiT-XL/2 at 256 px, the non-uniform part of early attention is spread over the whole 16×16 grid, not concentrated in a small window. The functional truncation test below qualifies this.

## Truncation test (two-part kernel: least-squares floor + residual inside K × K)

Per (window, block, head): f_K = mean of κ outside the centered K × K square; κ_K = κ inside, f_K outside; rebuild the row-normalized map and take its cosine to M (median over rows). K_min = smallest K with cosine ≥ 0.98. `kernel_truncation.json`.

| branch | median cosine K = 3 / 9 / 13 / 21 / 31 (full) | K_min median / p90 | K_min ≤ 9 | ≤ 15 | ≤ 21 | full kernel < 0.98 | K_min within 0.005 of full (median) | residual low-freq ≥ 0.9 |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| cond | 0.902 / 0.957 / 0.967 / 0.974 / 0.979 | none / none | 30% | 34% | 41% | 52% | 21 | 27% |
| uncond | 0.916 / 0.960 / 0.969 / 0.976 / 0.982 | 31.0 / none | 32% | 37% | 44% | 46% | 21 | 29% |

The distribution is bimodal. About 30% of kernels reach 0.98 with K ≤ 9, most already at K = 3. About half never reach 0.98 even with the full kernel, whose median cosine sits at the threshold. So an absolute 0.98 bar mostly measures the kernel form's own fit, not truncation.

**Functional check** (`truncation_smoke.json`; k = 10, seeds 0–31, rel. latent MSE vs FP): per-step maps 0.0161; V3 full 31 × 31 kernel 0.0219; two-part K = 15 0.0279; two-part K = 9 0.0286; uniform 0.2636. In image space, a floor plus a 9 × 9 residual is close to the full kernel (+0.0067), while the floor alone is not (+0.2417). Not FID-checked.

**The floor alone does not work; the residual is required.** The uniform-map control at k10 scored +20.97 FID-2k and -21.7 label-accuracy points against FP, far outside the k10 bar of +1.0 (no k15 uniform arm was run).

## Not verified

- Video DiTs.
- Any resolution other than 256 px, and so the kernel-support scaling the speed case depends on.
- Other samplers and step counts (all results use DDIM-50, cfg 1.5).
